# SheEarns Initiative (@sheearnsinitiative) captions

## 01. You do not have to know everything before you start

You see a job opening and think, "I am not ready yet." Confidence often grows after you begin, not before.

## 02. Your skill needs proof

You say you know social media management, but a potential client asks what you have done. Give people something they can look at.

## 03. You can start with the phone you have

It is easy to believe you need a perfect laptop and setup before learning. Do not let an imperfect setup stop every first step.

## 04. Mentorship is not someone doing the work for you

You finally meet someone experienced and want them to tell you exactly what to do. Good mentorship works best when you participate.

## 05. What if you have no work experience?

You want to apply for an opportunity, but your CV feels empty. You cannot invent experience, but you can explain the experience you have.

## 06. Learning a skill is not the same as earning from it

You finish a course and wait for someone to discover you. Learning opens a door. Showing your work helps people find you.

## 07. Your first price does not have to be your forever price

You are nervous about charging because you are just starting. Start fairly, then improve your offer and pricing with evidence.

## 08. Do not wait until your CV is perfect

You have edited the same CV twenty times but have not applied anywhere. A CV cannot open a door if it never leaves your phone.

## 09. One useful skill can lead to another

You begin with data entry and realise you enjoy organising information. You do not have to figure out your entire career in one day.

## 10. Your portfolio can start with practice work

You have no paying client yet, so you assume you cannot have a portfolio. Do not pretend it was paid work. Show what you can do.

## 11. Ask better questions when you are learning

You are stuck on a task and keep saying, "I do not understand anything." Learning gets easier when you can describe the problem.

## 12. A rejection is information, not your whole story

You applied and got a no. It stings, especially when you really wanted the role. Take the lesson if there is one. Do not turn it into your identity.

## 13. Professionalism starts before your first big job

You may be working on a small project, but the client still needs updates. Small opportunities are still places to build trust.

## 14. Do you know what you want to be hired for?

Your bio says you can do everything from design to bookkeeping to marketing. Make it easy for the right person to understand your offer.

## 15. Your next opportunity may start with one conversation

You assume networking means attending expensive events and collecting contacts. People remember curiosity and follow-through more than a perfect introduction.
